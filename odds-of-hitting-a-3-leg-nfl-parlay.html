The odds of hitting a 3-leg NFL parlay

If every leg is a coin flip, a three-leg parlay wins 1 time in 8. Whether that is a fair deal depends entirely on what the ticket pays.

The basic math

A parlay wins only if every leg wins, so you multiply the probabilities of the individual legs. Take three legs that each have a 50 percent chance. The result is 0.5 x 0.5 x 0.5, which is 12.5 percent, or one win in every eight tickets.

Most NFL point spreads and totals are priced at -110, which sounds like a coin flip but is not quite one. A -110 price implies a 52.4 percent chance (110 divided by 210), because the sportsbook builds a margin into both sides. We cover that margin on the true probability page. For now, the useful fact is that the book needs you to win less than 52.4 percent to make money on that leg, and the fair chance on a typical -110 side is closer to 50 percent.

What a 3-leg parlay pays

Parlay payouts multiply the decimal odds of each leg. A -110 bet has decimal odds of about 1.909. Three of them multiply to roughly 6.96, which means a $100 ticket returns $696 in total, a profit of $596. Sportsbooks quote this as +596.

That payout implies a break-even chance of 1 divided by 6.96, or 14.4 percent. You need to win 14.4 percent of the time to come out even. A fair three-leg ticket of coin flips wins 12.5 percent of the time. The gap between 14.4 and 12.5 is the price of playing.

Expected value of the typical ticket. At a true 12.5 percent win chance and a +596 payout, a $100 ticket is expected to lose about $13 on average. That is a 13 percent cost, compared with about 4.5 percent for a single -110 bet. Parlays multiply the sportsbook's margin along with the payout.

Look at the 52.4 percent row. A leg has to be a genuine 52.4 percent proposition, not 50, before the ticket breaks even. That is a modest edge on any single bet and a very hard one to find on three bets at once.

Why adding legs makes it worse

Each new leg multiplies your chance of winning by a number below one, so the win rate falls fast. With every leg at 50 percent, one leg wins half the time, two legs win a quarter of the time, three legs win 12.5 percent, four legs win 6.25 percent, and five legs win about 3 percent. The payout grows too, but the sportsbook's margin grows with it. The three-leg ticket is a middle ground, which is part of why it is so common.

Same-game legs are different

If two or three legs come from one game, they are linked. A quarterback going over his passing yards makes his receiver's over more likely and makes the game total over more likely. Books know this and price same-game parlays with the correlation in mind, often paying less than the simple multiplication would suggest. Do not use the 12.5 percent shortcut for a ticket built from one game.

What to take from this

A three-leg parlay is a reasonable entertainment purchase and a poor default strategy. If you play them, you want each leg to be better than the price says, and you want to know the combined chance before you compare it with the payout.
